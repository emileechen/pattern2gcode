Progress is also saved automatically in this browser and offered back when you reopen the same PDF. The project file does not contain the PDF itself.

2 · Detect pieces

Every closed outline becomes a piece. Tap a piece on the canvas to select it: the bar above the canvas lets you keep or drop it, rename it, set its grain, and nudge its outline points by hand. Labels, logos and stray shapes get dropped that way (names become file names).
Reads the label printed inside each piece (e.g. 2d) and names the piece after it. Loads the Tesseract OCR engine (~10 MB) from the internet the first time. Pieces you have renamed yourself are left alone.

3 · Pieces

Steps through every kept piece whose grain arrow hasn't been checked: look at the arrow, press Enter to confirm and move on, or use Set grain to tap it in yourself.

4 · Printer & fabric

Fabric setup: tape the fabric taut on all four sides, set Z offset ≈ fabric thickness, keep bed heat off or low. No purge line is generated (fabric covers the bed) — extrude a little by hand just before starting so the nozzle is primed.

5 · Export

Each internal line that reaches the outline is continued straight on past the cut line, so you can see where it runs and at what angle. The part outside is scrap; the overlap inside sits in the seam allowance.
Grain arrows are detected inside each piece (blue arrows on the canvas; green if set by hand). Each piece is rotated so its grain runs along the bed's Y axis — lay your fabric with its grain along Y every time. Pieces marked "no grain found" won't be rotated: check them and use the Grain button to tap the arrow's two ends yourself. When packing is on, kept pieces are grouped onto as few beds as possible (one G-code file per bed, plus a matching SVG layout sheet). Pieces larger than the bed are skipped and flagged.
